> Here, the second piece (again, the one with the code) feels more complex and interesting to me.
> The first one is quite similar to the second piece from the previous comparison. It has a basic counterpoint and rarely shows any sense of surprise or development. Because the counterpoint is mainly first-species, operating within a very generalized harmonic progression, it feels more like an “exercise” than a composition, and it doesn't really communicate any personality.

I also found it curious that the word “breathe” appears four times (what is it trying to say?)
> Although the melody and accompaniment themselves don't seem particularly interesting compared to the other pieces, and the music still suffers somewhat from the same first-species counterpoint problem, I find the second piece more expressive because of these extra decisions, especially the instrumentation and the text.

## Code vs ABC — Model A — Express yourself

- Piece 1: gemini-3.7-flash · codegen · “Solitude in Autumn Mist” (express-yourself, sample 4)
- Piece 2: gemini-3.7-flash · abc · “Echoes of the Unspoken” (express-yourself, sample 16)

**Window notes** *(blind)*:

> The first piece feels like it has more of an identity to me, and it seems to be trying to have a more personal quality compared to the second one.
> The first thing that comes to my mind is the relationship between the title and the music. Usually, the title is kind of the bridge between the artistic idea and how that idea becomes expressed in the music. In the first piece, I feel like there is actually a connection between the concept suggested by the title and the musical elements. For example, already in the first two bars, the harmony creates a more distinctive sound world, with the movement between D minor and G major (kind of modal)
> The melody also has this kind of vague quality. The first three notes don't really establish a clear tonality, which creates a feeling of uncertainty. I think this works quite well with the idea of solitude suggested by the piece. At the same time, the melody never really repeats itself, so it loses a bit of its identity or consistency. But I actually think the piece gains something from developing in less expected ways.
> The second piece feels more predictable to me. It uses a lot of sequences (which is a very common Baroque technique). Because there is such a clear mathematical pattern behind it, the development can feel a bit easier to anticipate (?) and maybe also a bit easier as a compositional strategy. The harmony also stays within a more conventional language.
> So overall, the first piece feels more exploratory and personal to me, while the second feels more based on established techniques and patterns.
