Self-preference v3 corpus · 52 judges × 2,011 pieces · 104,565 verdicts

Do LLMs like their own music? Every one of the 52 v3 arms (26 models, each with and without thinking) judged every express-yourself piece — ABC and code-gen — on the blind rubric1, including its own. A judge's self-preference is how much higher it scores its own pieces than the rest of the panel does, minus how much higher it scores everyone's pieces (its leniency). Positive = favors its own work beyond its general generosity; negative = harsher on itself. Quality = mean of the eight craft dimensions. Generated by llm-music selfpref-v3-report.

Most models do not reliably favor their own music, but a distinct minority do — strongly. Across 52 judge arms the mean corrected self-preference is +0.09 points on the 1–5 scale; 20 arms are significantly self-favoring and 12 significantly self-critical (95% bootstrap CIs over pieces). Most self-favoring: gemini-4-argon-thinking (+1.21), gemini-4-argon (+0.67), grok-4.3-thinking (+0.63). Most self-critical: gpt-6.1-sol-thinking (-0.25), gemini-3-flash-thinking (-0.24), gpt-6-astra-thinking (-0.23). Self-preference tracks judge incompetence: judges whose scores agree less with the rest of the panel favor themselves more (r = -0.39). Turning thinking on shifts self-preference by +0.06 on average across the 26 families, while a family's base and thinking arms still resemble each other (r = +0.76).

Self-preference and judge competence

Competence is how well a judge's scores track the rest of the panel (leave-one-out r). It correlates with self-preference at r = -0.39: the judges that agree least with everyone else are the ones that like themselves most. Leniency correlates at r = -0.03; how good a model's own pieces are (panel mean) at r = -0.06 — self-preference is not just strong composers correctly recognizing strong work. Judges below r = 0.75: grok-4.3 (0.65), grok-4.3-thinking (0.71), kimi-k2-thinking (0.70).

Methods, coverage and caveats
  • Data. The v3 express-yourself prompt only: 1,040 ABC pieces (52 arms × 20) and 971 code-gen pieces (69 code-gen generations failed and never existed). Each piece was judged by all 52 arms with the v3 judge prompt (blind, titles/notes stripped): 104,565 of 104,572 verdicts. (7 missing: fable-5-thinking on one opus-4.1-thinking ABC piece, where Anthropic's API returns stop_reason: refusal with zero output tokens, and 6 kimi-k2 and kimi-k2-thinking failures on code-gen pieces, not retried.)
  • Estimator. For judge J on piece i, gap = qJ(i) − mean of the other 51 judges on i, with q the mean of the eight craft dimensions. Leniency = mean gap over pieces by other families; strict self = mean gap over J's own pieces − leniency; sibling and family analogously. CIs: 1,000 bootstrap resamples of pieces (self set and leniency set resampled independently). This is the within-piece paired form of the leniency correction (score minus what the same piece got from everyone else), which controls for piece-mix difficulty — a judge whose own pieces happen to be good is not credited for it.
  • Provenance. Anthropic-native, Gemini and kimi-k3 arms were judged through provider batch APIs, the rest realtime, all with byte-identical prompts. Gemini batches ran with the JSON response-format hint omitted (Google's batch service otherwise flattens nested JSON); every batch was validated on a 50-piece slice against the Anthropic panel before scaling, and every full batch re-checked before merging.
  • Baselines. The ten newest arms (fable-5.1, opus-5.5, gpt-6-astra, gpt-6.1-sol, gemini-4-argon, ±effort) wrote stronger pieces than most of the corpus. A judge that marks older pieces far below its peers then looks self-favoring on the main estimator: gemini-4-argon-thinking is +1.21 there and +0.78 against other families' new-arm pieces. The vs new column in the first table shows that comparison for every arm.
  • Integrity gate. When the newest arms' pieces were added, the 32 older batch-judged arms failed the automatic gate on those 400 pieces and were merged anyway. The gate assumes a full-range corpus; these pieces are narrow in quality (panel SD 0.22 vs 0.50) and mostly wistful, and passing judges reach 57–68% wistful on them too. Against the four merged Anthropic arms, gemini-3-flash ±, gemini-3.1-pro, gemini-3.5-flash and grok-4.3 ± stay below r = 0.35 on these pieces (0.59–0.83 on older ones). gpt-5, 5.1 and 5.4 ±, kimi-k2 ± and opus-4.1 ± were judged realtime without the gate; kimi-k2-thinking, gpt-5.1-thinking and opus-4.1-thinking are also below 0.35. Every older judge rates these pieces 0.3–1.0 above its usual level, which could be real quality or judge bias.
  • Caveats. Self-preference here is behavioral: it does not distinguish a model recognizing its own style from a model simply liking the style it also produces (the v1/v2 style-space page tackles that with taste vectors and matched lookalikes). Cross-family agreement is lower for some families (Gemini ≈ 0.6 vs Anthropic ≈ 0.9), so competence and self-preference are partly family effects. Emotion labels and reasoning traces are in the raw file but not analyzed here.
