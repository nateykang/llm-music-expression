Self-preference v3 corpus · 42 judges × 1,611 pieces · 67,660 verdicts

Do LLMs like their own music? Every one of the 42 v3 arms (21 models, each with and without thinking) judged every express-yourself piece — ABC and code-gen — on the blind rubric1, including its own. A judge's self-preference is how much higher it scores its own pieces than the rest of the panel does, minus how much higher it scores everyone's pieces (its leniency). Positive = favors its own work beyond its general generosity; negative = harsher on itself. Quality = mean of the eight craft dimensions. Generated by llm-music selfpref-v3-report.

Most models do not reliably favor their own music, but a distinct minority do — strongly. Across 42 judge arms the mean corrected self-preference is +0.08 points on the 1–5 scale; 14 arms are significantly self-favoring and 10 significantly self-critical (95% bootstrap CIs over pieces). The self-favoring cluster is dominated by Gemini and Grok thinking arms (gemini-3.7-flash-thinking and grok-4.3-thinking both ≈ +0.65, half a rubric point); the gpt-5.x thinking arms sit at the other end (gpt-5.6-thinking -0.23), and the Anthropic arms cluster near zero. Self-preference tracks judge incompetence: judges whose scores agree less with the rest of the panel favor themselves more (r = -0.37). Turning thinking on shifts self-preference by only +0.06 on average across the 21 families, but the effect is wildly heterogeneous — it widens the gap for some families and narrows it for others — while a family's base and thinking arms still resemble each other (r = +0.65).

Self-preference and judge competence

Competence is how well a judge's scores track the rest of the panel (leave-one-out r). It correlates with self-preference at r = -0.37: the judges that agree least with everyone else are the ones that like themselves most. Leniency correlates at r = +0.30; how good a model's own pieces are (panel mean) at r = -0.18 — self-preference is not just strong composers correctly recognizing strong work. Judges below r = 0.75: grok-4.3 (0.64), grok-4.3-thinking (0.71), kimi-k2-thinking (0.69).

Methods, coverage and caveats
  • Data. The v3 express-yourself prompt only: 840 ABC pieces (42 arms × 20) and 771 code-gen pieces (69 code-gen generations failed and never existed). Each piece was judged by all 42 arms with the v3 judge prompt (blind, titles/notes stripped): 67,660 of 67,662 verdicts (2 missing: verdicts a judge failed to return as parseable JSON after repeated attempts, batch and realtime).
  • Estimator. For judge J on piece i, gap = qJ(i) − mean of the other 41 judges on i, with q the mean of the eight craft dimensions. Leniency = mean gap over pieces by other families; strict self = mean gap over J's own pieces − leniency; sibling and family analogously. CIs: 1,000 bootstrap resamples of pieces (self set and leniency set resampled independently). This is the within-piece paired form of the leniency correction (score minus what the same piece got from everyone else), which controls for piece-mix difficulty — a judge whose own pieces happen to be good is not credited for it.
  • Provenance. Anthropic-native, Gemini and kimi-k3 arms were judged through provider batch APIs, the rest realtime, all with byte-identical prompts. Gemini batches ran with the JSON response-format hint omitted (Google's batch service otherwise flattens nested JSON); every batch was validated on a 50-piece slice against the Anthropic panel before scaling, and every full batch re-checked before merging.
  • Caveats. Self-preference here is behavioral: it does not distinguish a model recognizing its own style from a model simply liking the style it also produces (the v1/v2 style-space page tackles that with taste vectors and matched lookalikes). Cross-family agreement is lower for some families (Gemini ≈ 0.6 vs Anthropic ≈ 0.9), so competence and self-preference are partly family effects. Emotion labels and reasoning traces are in the raw file but not analyzed here.
