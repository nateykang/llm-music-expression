Multimodal what models say about their music vs what the score says

En-route vs post-hoc descriptions, 808 pieces × 13 composers. Each piece has two descriptions by the same model: the one written in the same response as the music (en route) and a fresh, stateless call given only the finished score, anonymously (post hoc) — the describing call is never told the piece is its own, and titles, comments, and credits are scrubbed so nothing of the composing call's framing leaks through. Both texts were rated blind by fable-5 and gpt-5.6-thinking on three anchored scales, and a per-piece contrast pass lists what each text says that the other doesn't.

The scales, in plain terms. Evaluative positivity (1–7): how positively the text talks about the piece's quality or craft — 1 = openly critical, 4 = describes without evaluating, 7 = effusive praise. Weakness admission (1–5): does the text acknowledge flaws, limitations, or unrealized ambitions — 1 = none at all. Affect valence1 (1–9): how positive the emotions the text says the music expresses — grief and dread low, joy and serenity high, 5 = neutral or ambivalent — independent of any claims about quality. Length adjustment (Δ len-adj): the per-piece delta is regressed on the log ratio of the two texts' word counts; the reported value is the intercept, i.e. the expected delta for a pair of equal-length texts. The one-sentence short descriptions are length-matched by construction, so they carry no adjusted column.

Two opposite effects, cleanly separated by construct. Post hoc is rated more evaluatively positive (Δ = -0.31 on the length-matched short text, p ≈ 1e-37): describing a finished artifact invites appraisal language, while en route spends its words on intent. But en route paints the emotional content rosier (Δ = +0.55, p ≈ 8e-22): the same notes get a sunnier story from the model that just wrote them. Weakness admission is floored in both arms (99% vs 96% of short texts at 1/5) — models don't note flaws even in music they don't know they wrote.

Only en route says:

  • opus-think [emotional self-attribution] The resolution conveys peace rather than triumph, turning introspection toward calm acceptance and gentle hope.
  • opus-think [intent / goal] The composer wanted the piece to feel honest, unhurried, and sincere rather than dramatic.
  • opus-think [intent / goal] The composer wanted to express how they experience thinking: starting from a small motif, letting it recur and recombine, and watching meaning emerge from repetition with subtle variation.

Only post hoc says:

  • opus-think [technical structure] The piece is in 4/4 time.
  • opus-think [technical structure] The opening section is eight bars long.
  • opus-think [technical structure] The melody moves largely by conjunct/stepwise motion with occasional expressive leaps, including octave-spanning gestures in the middle section.

The asymmetry in one line: en route narrates a self; post hoc reads a score. En-route-only content is intent, imagery, and emotional self-attribution; post-hoc descriptions contribute ~11 unique technical statements per piece. The ~4 technical claims per piece unique to en route are candidate phantom claims — asserted by the composer but not recovered by an independent reading of the score.

Methods fine print

Ratings: one call per (piece, arm, rater) covering short and long text together; reason-before-score4; scales anchored (evaluative positivity 1–7 with 4 = purely descriptive; weakness admission 1–5; affect valence 1–91 with 5 = neutral). Raters saw the text only — no model name, title, or arm label. Rater agreement (Pearson): evaluative positivity 0.73, weakness admission 0.57, affect valence 0.92. fable-5 and gpt-5.6-thinking judge only in the free-form corpus; fable-5 composes 15 sparse-corpus pieces and rates its own text there (gpt-5.6-thinking, which composes nowhere, covers the bias check). Contrast pass: both arms shown as anonymous texts A/B (assignment randomized per piece), rater fable-5, fixed category taxonomy5.

Length control: the short description is a single sentence in both arms by construction and is the primary endpoint; long-text deltas are also reported at equal lengths via the intercept of Δ regressed on log word-ratio. One of 809 pieces failed post-hoc generation (JSON parse) and is excluded. Raw data: valence_comparison.json, description_contrast.json, description_arms_summary.json.
