How LLMs judge music — and themselves

An LLM-as-judge layer — language models, given a fixed rubric, standing in for human raters1 — over the generated pieces: blind quality + emotion ratings, each model's competence as a critic, and its self-bias. Rubric dimensions follow the music-eval literature; the protocol (reason-before-score, anchored scales, blind panel) follows the LLM-judge literature. Scope: 1500 free-form pieces. Generated by llm-music judge-report.

Which models write the best music (blind 15-model panel)

Every roster model judges every piece — a full 15×15 author-judge matrix, blind: the judge sees stripped notation only (no title, composer note, or model name), and each author's ranking below averages the OTHER models' verdicts (self-verdicts are held out for the self-bias analyses). Dimensions follow the music-eval literature (ChatMusician2 / Chu et al.3 / MuSpike4); scoring follows the LLM-judge literature1 — reason-before-score5, anchored 1–5 scales, plus a top-line holistic score asked last.

Judges' minor-affinity tracks their own minor-production rate at r = +0.56 (n=15 judges): models tend to favor music written in the modes they compose in themselves. Caveat: mode and author are not independent in this corpus (major pieces come overwhelmingly from the majority-major authors), so taste for an author's style can masquerade as mode affinity — a transposed/mode-flipped control set is the clean follow-up.

Footnotes & references

  1. Lianmin Zheng et al., “Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena”, NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2306.05685. “LLM-as-judge” = using a language model, given a rubric, as the rater instead of human annotators.
  2. Ruibin Yuan et al., “ChatMusician: Understanding and Generating Music Intrinsically with LLM”, 2024. arXiv:2402.16153.
  3. Hyeshin Chu et al., “An Empirical Study on How People Perceive AI-generated Music”, CIKM 2022. doi:10.1145/3511808.3557235. Source of the melodiousness / naturalness / creativity / coherence axes in the judging rubric.
  4. Qian Liang, Menghaoran Tang, Yi Zeng, “MuSpike: A Benchmark and Evaluation Framework for Symbolic Music Generation with Spiking Neural Networks”, 2025. arXiv:2508.19251. Source of several harmony/structure metrics and rubric dimensions used here.
  5. Yang Liu et al., “G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment”, EMNLP 2023. arXiv:2303.16634. Source of the reason-before-score protocol.
  6. James A. Russell, “A circumplex model of affect”, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 39(6), 1980. doi:10.1037/h0077714. The model places every emotion on two axes: valence (unpleasant ↔ pleasant) and arousal (calm ↔ activated).
